How reliable these numbers are

    Method and sources

    • Households: Census Bureau American Community Survey, 2024 one-year microdata for Florida, with incomes and home values brought forward to 2026.
    • Tax law: 2026 Florida assessment and exemption rules (homestead exemptions, Save Our Homes and the 10% non-homestead cap, senior, veteran and disability exemptions), each tied to its statute in the model's rules file.
    • Checked against the property rolls: on 9.85 million residential parcels in the 2026 Department of Revenue rolls, the rules reproduce the official taxable value exactly for 99.4%. Modelled homestead tax averages within 1% of the rolls, and within 4% for every home-value band from $100,000 to $2 million.
    • Save Our Homes: how far each home's assessed value sits below market value is drawn from real homestead parcels in the same area and value range, matched to owners using how long they have lived there and the tax they report.
    • Rates: 2026 school rates from the state's required local effort; county rates as adopted where collected, otherwise estimated from Florida's rate-setting rules; 2026 city rates for 91 cities (from county property appraisers and city records), other cities at 2025 levels.
    • Income groups: tenths of all Florida households by 2026 household income, renters included, so the groups will not move when renters are added.
    • Margins of error: 90% intervals from the survey's 80 replicate weights.

    What drives these projections

    How to read the range

      Method and sources

      • Current law only. Florida's assessment rules, exemptions and rate limits as written for 2026, with the Save Our Homes cap following inflation and the indexed exemption amounts growing with it. No future law changes, including Amendment 3 on the November 2026 ballot.
      • Home values: the state Revenue Estimating Conference's August 2026 forecast for existing residential property through 2032, then easing to the Florida Economic Estimating Conference's sale-price path (about 2% a year) by 2036.
      • Incomes and households: Florida Economic Estimating Conference (July 2026) wage, income and household forecasts; Social Security and pensions grow with inflation (CBO).
      • Sales and resets: homes change hands about 5.5% a year (homesteads), less often the larger their Save Our Homes savings, calibrated to the state's forecast of homestead assessment differences; deaths of owners living without a spouse are part of this. 14% of homestead sellers carry over savings (portability).
      • Tax rates: each county, city and school district's yearly rate change is drawn from what Florida governments of the same kind did in years with similar growth (1996-2025), within the 2026 law's limit of 110% of the rolled-back rate.
      • New owners: owner households grow with Florida's household forecast; new owners live in new homes taxed on full value, placed where new construction happens.
      • Population: owners shift toward older ages following Florida's official population projections by age.
