# Texas property tax proposals, 2025-2026: provisions to encode

Compiled 2026-10-21. Covers six items: Abbott's five-step plan; Hinojosa; Patrick's "Operation
Double Nickel"; the 2026 Republican Party of Texas (RPT) platform; the 2026 Texas Democratic Party
(TDP) platform; and official cost figures.

**Status:** none of these is law or a filed bill. Bill prefiling for the 90th Legislature opens
in November 2026, and the session begins **January 12, 2027**. Every proposal below needs
legislation, and most need a constitutional amendment (joint resolution, two-thirds of each house,
then a statewide vote). A joint resolution passed in the 2027 regular session would normally go
to voters in November 2027. The first tax year affected would then be 2027 (if made retroactive,
as in 2023 and 2025) or 2028.

**Sources:** primary sources were read where possible: the Abbott campaign PDF (an image, read
visually), the RPT and TDP platform PDFs (text extracted), the Bettencourt Senate press release,
the Lt. Governor's interim-charge PDF and press release, and the House Ways & Means hearing notice.
Several news sites (KXAN, Houston Chronicle, Dallas Morning News, Texas Tribune direct,
Texas Monthly) blocked fetching. Their content is taken from republications (KSAT/KUT for the
Tribune, Community Impact, CBS Texas, Texas Scorecard, Bond Buyer) or from search snippets, and is
flagged where it matters.

## 1. Governor Greg Abbott: "Empowering Texas Taxpayers: Five Steps to Overhaul the Property Tax System"

- **Primary source:** campaign PDF (two image pages; upload path dated 2025-11; "Pol. Ad. Texans for
  Greg Abbott"): https://www.gregabbott.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Empowering-Texas-Taxpayers.pdf
- **Also:** https://www.gregabbott.com/policy/ (same text)
- **Dates:** announced at the campaign kickoff, Houston, early November 2025. He signed the
  "Taxpayer Empowerment Pledge" in Frisco on Nov. 20, 2025 (Bond Buyer). It was repeated in the
  Report to the People on May 6, 2026. The RPT SREC endorsed it in a resolution posted March 20,
  2026. 88-89 GOP House incumbents and nominees signed the pledge (announced May 18, 2026; updated
  June 30, 2026).

### Provisions

| # | Step | Exact wording (campaign PDF) | Units | Property | Encoding notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Local spending limit | "population + inflation or 3.5% (the lesser of the two)" | "cities, counties, and other local government entities". Schools are not named; "other local" is open | n/a (spending) | Cap year-over-year growth in spending (or levy, as a proxy) at min(pop growth + CPI, 3.5%). The base is **spending**, not levy, and "spending" is not defined (all funds? M&O only? debt?). It also extends the "state's debt limit and prohibition on deficit spending" to all levels of government. |
| 2 | Two-thirds voter approval | "two-thirds voter approval on all local property tax increases" | all local units | all | Today the voter-approval-rate (VAR) election needs a simple majority. Under this step, a rate above the trigger needs 2/3 of voters. "Tax increase" is not defined: the current VAR, the no-new-revenue (NNR) rate, or any levy growth? Texas Scorecard says it covers bonds too. Model it as: any rate above the trigger is very unlikely to pass, or set a pass probability. |
| 3 | Voter rollback petitions | "if 15% of registered voters in a local area sign a petition, they can force a rollback election to lower rates" | all local units | all | The rollback target rate is unspecified (to the NNR rate? to the VAR?). This is a stochastic or scenario lever. Current law has petition-triggered elections only in narrow cases. |
| 4a | Reappraisal frequency | "all properties be appraised only once every five years" | appraisal districts (all units) | **all property** | Hold appraised value fixed for 5 years, then reappraise. Unspecified: whether the cycle is staggered or statewide, whether new improvements are added between cycles (presumably yes), and how the reappraisal interacts with the 3% cap. A strict reading is a 5-year freeze plus a 3% cap at reappraisal, so the step-up is at most 3% a year compounded. |
| 4b | Appraisal cap | "lower the homestead appraisal cap from 10% to 3%" | all units | homesteads | Tax Code 23.23 cap: 10% to 3%. |
| 4c | Cap extension | "expand that cap to all properties, which will help renters and businesses" | all units | **all property** (Yahoo/Abbott summary says "including rental and commercial") | It replaces the 20% circuit-breaker on non-homestead real property of $5.16M or less (23.231), which expires at the end of 2026, and extends to all property. Unclear whether it covers business personal property, minerals, or ag/open-space (already on productivity value). Unclear whether the cap is per year or per 5-year cycle. |
| 5 | School tax elimination | "voters will have the right to decide - through a constitutional amendment - to eliminate school district property taxes on homeowners" | school districts | **homesteads only**. Abbott said rentals and businesses keep paying (Texas Tribune, Dec 9 2025) | The amendment is the vehicle. M&O vs I&S is **not specified**; see ambiguities. |

### Other statements by Abbott
- "The state should pay for public education, not your homestead" (Report to the People, May 6, 2026).
- The school district bill "should be zero dollars" (Pasadena event, reported Oct 7, 2026). This
  wording implies the I&S portion too.
- Removing school taxes "will cut the average homestead's property taxes by half" (campaign site).

### Funding
- No funding source appears in the campaign document.
- In statements, Abbott says the cost would be paid from the **recurring state budget surplus**. He
  says no sales tax increase is needed: "There's no reason to raise our sales tax" (Tribune,
  Dec 2025).
- His own cost figure: "this is going to cost about $10 billion" (Pasadena; KPRC, Oct 7, 2026). He
  added that "the budget surplus alone would have been enough". He also mentioned using "some other
  reserve money" to keep relief going (Community Impact).
- Comptroller staff put the homestead school **M&O** cost at $8.6B a year now and $12B by 2032 (see
  section 6). Abbott's ~$10B is close to that. It may include I&S or growth; this is unverified.

### What is left to legislation or unspecified
- Whether I&S (debt) taxes on homesteads are eliminated. Bond Buyer reports that the campaign did
  not answer how bond debt would be treated.
- The mechanism: a 100% homestead exemption for school taxes, or a state-paid credit. A 100%
  exemption shifts nothing onto other property only if state aid replaces the lost revenue dollar
  for dollar. If districts can raise rates on non-homestead property, there is a shift.
- Phase-in: none is stated. The tone implies one step after a constitutional vote. Pat O'Connor
  says it is "highly unlikely" in the 2027 session.
- Treatment of the 65+/disabled tax ceiling, which becomes moot.
- Enrichment (golden and copper pennies) and local choice above the MCR. Do homesteads also stop
  paying voter-approved enrichment? Presumably yes ("zero dollars").
- Recapture: whether the lost homestead levy is backfilled through the Foundation School Program
  (FSP) hold-harmless, as SB 2/HB 2 (2023) and SB 4/SB 23 (2025) did.
- Spending limit: definition of "spending", the population and inflation measures, and the remedy
  for an exceedance (voter override?).
- The rollback election target rate and the petition window.
- 5-year appraisal: the cycle design, new construction, and changes in ownership.

## 2. Gina Hinojosa (Democratic nominee for governor, 2026)

### Provisions

| Item | What the campaign says | Encoding notes |
|---|---|---|
| Goal | "end goal of eliminating residential property taxes" (ginafortexas.com, "Money in Your Pocket") | "Residential", not "homestead". It may include rentals. It is not limited to school taxes, so read literally it covers all units. |
| Near-term action | "end these corporate subsidies and use the savings to cut property taxes" | No amount, no mechanism, and no named programs (JETI / Ch. 403, the Texas Enterprise Fund, and the Ch. 313 tail are the likely targets; **unverified**). |
| Conditions | Cut taxes without hurting the economy, schools, or working families (Sept 2026 press release); "fully fund schools and cut taxes" | n/a |
| Hedge | Voter guide: elimination is the "ultimate goal", but she "won't promise something I can't deliver" | It is an aspiration, not a schedule. |
| Separate item | One-time **$1,500 per household** check from the Rainy Day Fund ($17B of a ~$27B balance, leaving ~$10B) | Not a property tax change. It could be modelled as a lump-sum transfer for comparison. $17B / $1,500 is about 11.3M households (my arithmetic). |

### Funding
- Savings from ending corporate subsidies, plus cutting "corrupt" state contracts (Houston
  Chronicle headline via search; article blocked).
- No dollar figure.
- Scale check: the Dallas Morning News editorial board puts residential property taxes at "some
  $50 billion a year" (citing Comptroller reports). That is far more than any plausible
  corporate-subsidy savings.

### Unspecified
- Everything needed to encode it: which units, homestead vs all residential, the phase-in, the
  amount per year, and the mechanism (exemption, rate cut, or state credit).
- The Texas Tribune voter guide (Oct 8, 2026) calls it "unspecified".
- **Recommendation:** model only as illustrative scenarios (for example, revenue X from ending
  subsidies spread as a uniform homestead exemption or as a school M&O rate cut), clearly labelled
  as an interpretation.

## 3. "Operation Double Nickel" (Lt. Gov. Dan Patrick)

- **Who:** Lt. Gov. Dan Patrick (press conference, Texas Capitol, **Dec 9, 2025**). It is backed by
  Sen. Paul Bettencourt (R-Houston), chair of Senate Local Government.
- **Bill:** none yet. Patrick lists it among his top 2027 priorities.
- **Interim charge:** Senate **Finance** Committee, Round 1 interim charges, **Jan 30, 2026**,
  "Further Property Tax Cuts": "Assess the impact of reducing the senior homestead exemption from
  65 to 55 years of age." The charge says this would "freeze home values 10 years sooner for over 3
  million homeowners". A Finance hearing on Sept 2, 2026 that listed the charge was **cancelled**.
  No later hearing was found.

### Provisions

| Item | Current law (TY2025+) | Double Nickel | Units | Encoding notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| General school homestead exemption (Tax Code 11.13(b)) | $140,000 | **$180,000** (+$40,000) | school districts only | Straightforward. |
| Additional 65+/disabled exemption (11.13(c)) | $60,000 (total $200,000) | Total **$240,000**. The KUT/Tribune piece says relief would total $240,000 at 55. | school districts | This implies the additional exemption stays at $60,000 on top of $180,000. **Ambiguous** whether disabled people keep it and whether age 55-64 gets the full $60,000 extra (CBS: "55-65 go from $140,000 to $240,000"). Encode as age >= 55: $180k + $60k. |
| Age for the senior exemption | 65 | **55** | school districts | Also lower the age for the surviving-spouse carryover (11.13(q))? Unspecified; assume the age is 55 throughout. |
| School tax "freeze" (11.26 tax ceiling) | Tax ceiling set at the tax paid in the first year a person qualifies at 65 (adjusted down by later compression and exemption increases) | Ceiling starts at 55 | school districts (M&O + I&S under 11.26) | Patrick and Bettencourt say "values are frozen" and "locks in their values". The Tribune and current law describe a **tax ceiling** ("caps the amount of school property taxes paid... upon turning 65"). The proposal extends the existing mechanism, so **encode as the 11.26 tax ceiling with the qualifying age 55**, not a value freeze. Flag the wording. |
| Count | about 6.1M homesteads | "3.3M homeowners" frozen (Bettencourt); "over 3 million" (charge) | n/a | Check against the model: 55+ homestead heads plus the current 65+. |
| Local government | n/a | "reining in local government budget growth"; Scorecard: a 3.5% cap on city and county budget increases | cities, counties | Mechanism unspecified. Overlaps Abbott step 1. |
| Elimination path | n/a | "Once we eliminate all school taxes... in two or three sessions total" (CBS, Dec 2025), through repeated exemption increases | school districts, homesteads | Not a specific provision. It could be modelled as a schedule of exemption increases. |

### Cost
- Patrick: the package costs **under $4 billion per biennium** (Texas Scorecard, Dec 9, 2025 and
  June 30, 2026). Unclear whether that is the cost of the $40k plus age-55 changes together, and
  first or second year.
- Patrick: "For every billion dollars that we invest in homestead exemptions, you get about $130
  savings," versus about $30 per billion for compression (Bettencourt release). That is per average
  homestead.
- Savings: "nearly a $1,000 per year" for 55-64 homeowners (Bettencourt). Up to $10,000 over 10
  years (Scorecard).
- No LBB or Comptroller fiscal note was found. The Senate Finance hearing was cancelled.

### Funding
- State general revenue (FSP hold-harmless for districts, as with prior exemption increases). No
  new tax.

### Patrick on full elimination
- Eliminating school property taxes now would need a sales tax increase: "You'd have to go from
  eight and a quarter cents to 14 or 15 cents. You'd have to double the sales tax" (reporters,
  Dec 2025, reported by KXAN Dec 14, 2025; KXAN blocked, quote via search snippet).
- Note that 8.25% is the combined state (6.25%) plus maximum local (2%) rate.
- Eliminating **all** property taxes: the sales tax would need to be "at least 25 cents" per dollar
  (some reports say up to 26). "I don't think that's realistic."
- **No dollar cost** was found attached to his 15-cent figure. Placards at the Dec 9 press
  conference made the sales-tax point. Their exact numbers are not verified.

## 4. Republican Party of Texas: 2026 Platform and Resolutions

- **Source:** "2026 Platform and Resolutions of the Republican Party of Texas", as approved at the
  June 2026 state convention in Houston. The PDF was uploaded 2026-07:
  https://texasgop.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/2026-Republican-Party-of-Texas-Platform-As-Approved.pdf
  The text below is extracted from it.
- **Section:** "School Finance and Property Taxation", planks 79-80, plus plank 66 (spending) and
  plank 67 (ESF). The Resolutions section has no property tax items.

### Provisions

| Plank | Key wording (verbatim fragments) | Units | Property | Numbers / timing | Encoding notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 79 "Ax the Property Tax" | "eliminating the property tax system for businesses and individuals" | all | all private property ("including homesteads, fee simple, and land-patented lands") | "immediately develop and implement a transition plan that is a net tax cut" | No new "onerous taxes like an income tax". It excludes leases and permits on public land. |
| 80(a) Cost-basis valuation | "rolling values back to 2019 pre-COVID certified values" | "across all taxing entities" | "residential and commercial properties" | "annual cap of 3% increase ... for each year forward" | Taxable value = 2019 certified value x 1.03^(t-2019), or perhaps 3% from the start year. **Ambiguous:** whether the 3% compounds from 2019 (2026 = 1.23 x 2019) or values reset to 2019 and grow from enactment. Encode: each biennium, compress the MCR by 0.9 x surplus / M&O levy base. The plank 66 wording is the same ("beginning with buying down school maintenance and operations taxes"). |
| 67 ESF | ESF excess goes "first ... into the Texas Health Savings Account (HSA), and then go towards property tax relief" | state | n/a | 6 months after the triggering event | n/a |

### Related RPT items
- **2026 GOP primary, Proposition 1 (advisory, March 3, 2026):** "Texas property taxes should be
  assessed at the purchase price and phased out entirely over the next six years through spending
  reductions." It passed with **87.98%**. This is acquisition-value assessment (Prop-13 style), not
  the 2019 rollback in the platform. The two differ (5 vs 6 years; 2019 values vs purchase price).
- **Prop 2:** voter approval of any local budget that raises property taxes; **93.72%**.
- **SREC resolution** (posted Mar 20, 2026) endorses Abbott's five steps and calls for
  "prioritizing the elimination of school property taxes for all property owners".
- **Platform text not found:** the task brief mentions "Closing the unused increment rate" and
  "closing the disaster rate loophole". Both are confirmed as 80(c) and 80(d).

### Funding
- Spending restraint and freezes (79, 80(h)) and 90%+ of surpluses (66, 80(i)).
- Explicitly rules out an income tax.
- Silent on sales tax increases. Plank 79 says no "new, onerous taxes like an income tax", which
  does not literally rule out raising the sales tax.

### Ambiguities
- Internal inconsistency: 80(a) (abolish all property tax in 5 years) versus 80(i) (buy down school
  M&O with surpluses "until eliminated", with no deadline).
- The base year for the 3% growth.
- Whether the 2019 rollback applies to BPP, minerals, industrial and ag land.
- How the 5-year phase-out is shaped.

## 5. Texas Democratic Party: Platform 2026-2028

- **Source:** "Texas Democratic Party Platform 2026 - 2028" (final PDF, hosted by Progress Texas,
  2026-06): https://progresstexas.org/sites/default/files/2026-06/2026%20TDP%20Platform%20(Final)%20PDF.pdf
  The text was extracted. The planks are under "Lower Taxes" (affordability section, about p. 4),
  plus "Moratoriums on Data Centers" (about p. 24) and "Increasing Fiscal Responsibility".

### Provisions

| Plank | Verbatim fragment | Property / units | Encoding notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flat-dollar homestead exemption by petition | "right to petition and vote for a flat-dollar homestead exemption" | homesteads; local units (implied) | Paid for by "shifting the tax burden onto wealthy landlords". Current law allows only percentage exemptions for cities, counties and special districts (11.13(n)). This would let voters force a flat-dollar one. The amount is unspecified. It is revenue-neutral via a rate increase, falling on non-homestead property. "Wealthy landlords" could also mean a separate rental-property rate (would need a constitutional change; unspecified). |
| Renter homestead exemption | "Entitle renters with exchangeable homestead exemptions to secure rent relief" | rental residential | The mechanism is unspecified: a pass-through exemption on the landlord's parcel, or a credit? Note the model's "pass-through dropped" decision. |
| Temporary appraisal freeze | "Temporarily freeze rising residential property tax appraisals" | **residential** (not only homesteads) | Duration unspecified. School funding is preserved "from the Property Value Study", meaning state aid uses PVS values, not frozen local values. |
| Sales price disclosure | "disclose the final sale price of a property to the public" | all | An appraisal accuracy and data issue. Model it as reduced under-appraisal of commercial and high-value property, if at all. |
| Uniform appraisal | "commercial, industrial, and open space properties are taxed at market value" | commercial, industrial, ag/open-space | That would end or limit productivity valuation (1-d-1) and push commercial values up to market. It shifts the burden away from residential property. |
| Current-use valuation for residential | "current use rather than highest and best use, with claw-backs after the property is sold" | all residential | Hard to encode without a land-use model. The claw-back resembles a rollback tax on sale. |
| Data center reimbursements | "Reverse and prohibit future taxpayer-funded corporate welfare incentives, including local sales and property tax reimbursements for data centers" | data centers (abatements, Ch. 312/JETI/Ch. 381 agreements) | Raises taxable value in some districts. Needs data on abatement values. |
| Other | "Reduce tax incentives for businesses that shift tax burdens onto residents"; "End unfunded mandates by the State which drive up county property tax rates"; school finance "reduces overreliance on local property taxes" | n/a | Directional only. |

### Funding
- The flat-dollar exemption is self-funded by a shift onto landlords.
- No state cost figures. Elsewhere: tax billionaires, franchise tax reform, ending corporate
  incentives.

### Ambiguities
- Amounts for the flat exemption, the freeze length, and the renter mechanism are all unstated.
- The Democratic primary advisory ballot (March 2026) had no property tax proposition.
